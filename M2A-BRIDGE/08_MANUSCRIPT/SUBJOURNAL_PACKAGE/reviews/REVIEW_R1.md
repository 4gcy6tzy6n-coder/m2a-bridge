# Reviewer 1 (lens: computational soundness)

## Overall assessment

This manuscript couples a delayed flight-steering model study with four public-dataset analyses, and its central computational claim is a necessity statement: the ingredient that stabilizes delayed visual steering is fresh current self-state routed through a fast feedback path, with pathway gating making an independent contribution, while delayed-only estimators and matched-budget classical control fail (Abstract; "Classical control alternatives do not explain the rescue"; claims C37–C41 in the claim-evidence map). The unusual strengths of this packet are its negative-result hygiene: window artifacts are self-identified and retracted (C23), criterion sensitivity is quantified (C10, C33), the source model's ranking is honestly declared non-reproduced (C12), and the claim map retires superseded claims rather than deleting them. Within its stated construction — a transport delay applied to bar angle, grating angle, heading, and angular velocity, with body and torque filters current ("Delay" section) — the simulation results appear internally consistent as reported.

However, viewed strictly through the validity of the necessity claims and the adequacy of the observer and classical-control comparisons, the central case is not yet established. The two comparison arms on which the necessity claim most depends — the "Smith-style" forward-model observer and the "matched-budget instantaneous classical control" — are each compromised in ways the claim map itself partly concedes (C39: "internal model omits the delay in its feedback loop (naive forward model); DDE-aware observer untested"; C40: "PD not gain-tuned (matched-budget design)"). In addition, the one classical controller tested was never paired with the very mechanism (gating) that the paper shows is decisive, and the delay construction itself withholds current heading and angular velocity from every pathway except the rescue branch — which shapes what the necessity map can conclude. I recommend major revision: the work is careful and potentially publishable at Nature Machine Intelligence, but the title/abstract-level necessity claims currently outrun the comparator set.

## Who would be interested and why

- Researchers in closed-loop control under sensory delay (predictor feedback, Smith predictors, time-delay systems) will find the empirical failure of naive delayed-only estimation and the freshness dose-response (C36) of interest — and will immediately ask the DDE-aware-predictor question raised below.
- ML researchers working on embodied agents, latency in reinforcement-learning control loops, and dual-rate or reactive-vs-deliberative architectures will recognize the "fresh fast path plus gated delayed pathway" structure; the manuscript currently under-develops this transfer.
- Systems neuroscientists studying efference copy and corollary discharge will value the methodological result on pseudo-pulse controls and the residual pipeline (C45, C46), which generalizes to any state-dependent perturbation analysis, neural or behavioral.

## Major strengths

1. Exceptional negative-result and self-correction discipline. The window-artifact retraction ("The long-delay gating claim was a window artifact"; C23), the explicit retirement of the 200 ms critical-delay claim, and the criterion-sensitivity disclosures (C10, C33; "Failure criteria and windows") set a standard for computational reporting.
2. The command-copy control ("A true command copy does not rescue delay"; C18, C21) cleanly separates information content from signal timing — a genuine identification argument: the command copy adds no information beyond the delayed visual estimate, and this reproduces identically across seeds.
3. The freshness dose-response (Fig. 14a; C36) converts the qualitative claim into a quantitative, experimentally testable threshold (~20–30 ms), and the damping control (C35) shows the rescue needs no goal content — the right ablation logic for a necessity argument.
4. The pseudo-pulse control and residual pipeline (Empirical layer III; C45, C46) are, to my mind, the most transferable contribution of the paper: a quantified demonstration that a preregistered primary endpoint (5/6 animals, p=0.031) can be entirely an autocorrelation artifact, plus a partial fix with a preregistered specificity threshold that the authors honestly report as unmet.
5. The claim-evidence map (C1–C46) with strength qualifiers, caveats, and retired-claim statuses is exemplary supplementary material and made this review possible.

## Major Concerns

### R1-M1
- Severity: High
- Blocking: Yes
- Axis: Validity of the classical-control comparison
- Claim pointer: Abstract ("matched-budget instantaneous classical control does not [rescue]"); section "Classical control alternatives do not explain the rescue"; C40; supporting claim in Discussion ("Matched-budget classical proportional-derivative control with instantaneous state also failed, so the result is not merely 'instantaneous state helps'").
- Evidence pointer: C40 caveat "PD not gain-tuned (matched-budget design); claim limited to this comparator"; Methods addition "Classical-control comparators (model)" ("no delay in the steering feedback and no gating").
- Concern: The single classical comparator tested is an instantaneous PD bar controller with the optomotor pathway left ungated. Its failure in 38–62% of parameter sets is therefore confounded: it fails in the presence of a delayed, destabilizing, ungated grating loop — a condition under which even the model's own `addition` variant fails. This comparator does not isolate "classical control with instantaneous state fails"; it re-demonstrates that the delayed optomotor loop must be gated or cancelled. The decisive hybrid was never run: instantaneous PD with gating (or with the grating pathway removed). Note that the paper's own rescue architecture, `gate goal`, *is* a classical PD-on-current-state fast path (F_goal = P(goal − ψ) + (dθ_goal − ω)·V; "Methods: Model") combined with gating. Without a gated classical comparator, the claim that "the architecture (fast path plus gated slow pathway) matters" beyond classical means is not established — the rescue may be exactly classical PD plus gating, with the "classical failure" resting on an unfair ungated-grating condition.
- Why it matters: This is one of the three pillars in the abstract and the Discussion's main rebuttal to the "ordinary classical control would suffice" objection. If a gated classical PD also rescues (as the paper's own logic predicts), the necessity map must be rewritten: what is necessary is instantaneous state *and* suppression of the delayed pathway, and the fast path itself need not be non-classical.
- Resolution test: Run the crossed conditions at the same 6 s window, eight parameter sets, and multi-seed protocol: (i) instantaneous PD + gate on the grating pathway, (ii) instantaneous PD with grating pathway removed, (iii) optionally a gain-tuned PD (tuned on a held-out parameter subset). Either show they fail — in which case the current claim survives with real force — or report that they succeed and re-scope the abstract/Discussion accordingly. Also report the PD tuning procedure explicitly.

### R1-M2
- Severity: High
- Blocking: Yes
- Axis: Observer comparison / estimator elimination
- Claim pointer: Abstract ("Neither trivial extrapolation nor a full forward-model observer built solely from delayed observations rescues stability"); section "Classical control alternatives do not explain the rescue" ("a forward-model observer in the style of a Smith predictor"); Discussion ("Estimators built from delayed observations failed at every order we tested — from second-order extrapolation to a forward-model observer with the exact plant dynamics — so the gap between delayed observation and fresh state cannot be closed by estimation sophistication within this system"); C37, C39.
- Evidence pointer: C39 caveat verbatim: "internal model omits the delay in its feedback loop (naive forward model); DDE-aware observer untested"; C37 caveat: "only a trivial Taylor estimator; a perfect internal-model observer is an untested upper bound"; Results ("A perfect internal-model observer remains an untested upper bound").
- Concern: A Smith predictor in the classical sense uses an internal model *including* the dead time, with the delayed model output subtracted in the feedback path so the controller sees a delay-free prediction; predictor-feedback (Artstein) schemes stabilize pure-delay plants under model knowledge using only delayed measurements. The implemented observer "omits the delay in its feedback loop," which the map itself labels a "naive forward model" — this is not the classical construction it is styled after, and the necessity claim ("the gap … cannot be closed by estimation sophistication") is in direct tension with standard time-delay control theory unless the authors show why predictor feedback fails for this plant. The abstract's phrase "a full forward-model observer" also overstates what C39 admits was a naive one.
- Why it matters: The necessity of *fresh state* is exactly the claim that classical predictor theory denies in general. Nature Machine Intelligence reviewers will know this literature. As written, the negative result eliminates naive estimators, not the estimator class the claim's wording targets.
- Resolution test: Implement a delay-aware predictor (internal model with the transport delay in the feedback path, or an Artstein-type reduction) with exact plant parameters and the same mismatch condition; run at 50–200 ms, 6 s window, eight parameter sets, multi-seed. If it fails, the necessity claim gains its missing support and the abstract can keep its scope; if it succeeds (e.g., at exact parameters), the claim must be restated as "fresh state substitutes for model-based prediction under model mismatch," which would itself be a strong, well-scoped result. Alternatively, re-scope all necessity wording to "relative to the estimators tested (Taylor and a naive forward model)."

### R1-M3
- Severity: Medium
- Blocking: No
- Axis: Internal decomposition of the rescue (torque freshness vs. gate-trigger freshness)
- Claim pointer: Abstract ("pathway gating making an independent model-level contribution"); C41 ("at matched instantaneous state and goal branch, gated is 0% while ungated graded_goal is 62-75% failure"); C20; C35.
- Evidence pointer: Methods: Model — for gate goal, "`k_opto` is set to zero while `|F_bar + ff_gain * F_goal| > gate_threshold`" (the fresh self-state signal enters both the corrective torque and the gate trigger); Limitations: "the same augmented signal drives both the corrective torque and the gate trigger"; C35 caveat "damping matched by scale, not by RMS torque".
- Concern: Two identification gaps remain in the claim that gating contributes *independently*. First, C41 compares across architectures (gated family vs. graded family), not within one architecture by ablating the gate; the graded variant differs from the gated variant in the efference-copy mechanism as well as in gating. Second, within the rescue branch, current self-state simultaneously feeds the bar torque and the gate trigger, so the experiment does not decompose how much of the rescue is "fresh stabilizing torque" versus "fresh gate triggering." The `ff_self_state = delayed` ablation (C20) shows freshness matters *somewhere* in the branch, not which role carries it.
- Why it matters: The abstract presents two separable contributions (fresh state; independent gating). The within-model decomposition supporting the second, and the torque-vs-trigger attribution of the first, are currently under-identified.
- Resolution test: Two cheap ablations at the existing sweep sizes: (i) gate goal with the gate disabled (grating pathway always on) — isolates gating within the same architecture; (ii) a hybrid where F_goal enters the torque but the gate triggers on `|F_bar|` alone (and the converse). Report the four-cell decomposition.

### R1-M4
- Severity: Medium
- Blocking: No
- Axis: Delay construction shapes the necessity map
- Claim pointer: "A minimal steering model and five variants" ("The heading and angular velocity are treated as visually sensed body states; delaying them is an assumption, not a claim about proprioception"); Abstract's necessity framing; Prediction 6.
- Evidence pointer: Delay section ("evaluating the visual state at time t − d: the bar angle and its derivative, the heading, and the angular velocity"); Limitations ("Real sensorimotor delays are distributed and state-dependent").
- Concern: The transport-delay construction delays heading and angular velocity in *every* delayed pathway, while the rescue branch alone receives them fresh. If the biological system has current (proprioceptive or efference-related) access to ψ and ω in its fast pathways — which the manuscript concedes is plausible — then part of the model's failure structure is manufactured by the delay assignment itself: a bar pathway with instantaneous ψ/ω was never tested and might not fail at 50–100 ms. The necessity claim is valid as stated *for this construction*, but the construction is doing non-trivial work that is acknowledged in one sentence and never probed.
- Why it matters: The paper's forward reference to biology (Prediction 6: delay the self-state pathway, not the visual scene) depends on the assumption that the delayed pathway would not otherwise have current body-state access. A single ablation (delay only bar/grating angles; keep ψ/ω current in all pathways) would quantify how much of the necessity map is an artifact of withholding body-state feedback.
- Resolution test: Run the addition, graded filtered, and gate filtered variants with ψ/ω undelayed in the bar and grating pathways at 20–200 ms, 6 s window. Report whether the continuous variants' failure boundary shifts materially; include as a supplementary robustness sweep either way.

## Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established

1. The classical-control arm of the necessity argument is confounded: the only classical comparator is tested under an ungated delayed grating loop and was never crossed with gating (R1-M1). Until the gated-classical hybrid is run, the abstract's classical-control pillar and the Discussion's "not merely instantaneous state" inference do not follow from the simulations.
2. The observer arm eliminates a "naive forward model," not a Smith/predictor-feedback construction, and the claim map itself concedes "DDE-aware observer untested" (C39) — while the title/abstract/Discussion phrase the result as a general failure of delayed-only estimation (R1-M2). The gap between these two statements is where the central necessity claim currently lives.
3. The delay construction withholds current heading and angular velocity from all non-rescue pathways by assumption, and no robustness check probes how much of the failure structure this choice creates (R1-M4).

These are all resolvable with additional simulation sweeps of the kind the paper already runs routinely; none requires new data.

## Assessment against Nature Machine Intelligence criteria

- **Methodological rigor:** Above average for the field in its hygiene (preregistration with frozen hashes, negative controls run alongside every discrimination analysis, retired claims preserved in a map). Below the bar in the specific area of comparator design for the central claim (R1-M1, R1-M2).
- **Novelty:** The freshness dose-response threshold and the pseudo-pulse/residual methodology are novel contributions. The negative estimator results are only novel if the eliminated estimator class includes the classical delay-aware constructions.
- **Relevance to AI/ML and intelligent systems:** Currently moderate and under-articulated. Delayed-observation control, the insufficiency (or sufficiency) of prediction versus fresh sensing, and the autocorrelation confound in closed-loop evaluation are directly relevant, but the manuscript does not make the case for this audience (R1-m8). Scope discipline is otherwise commendable: the paper repeatedly and explicitly bounds claims as "internal to the model," with the notable exceptions of the title, the abstract's necessity sentence, and the Discussion's estimator-sophistication generalization.

## Recommendation posture

Major revision, with a path to acceptance. I would not support publication in the current form because two of the three abstract-level necessity claims rest on comparator arms the authors' own claim map identifies as incomplete (C39, C40). I would support publication if either outcome follows from the resolution tests: (a) the delay-aware observer and the gated-classical hybrid both fail, strengthening the necessity claim to match its current wording; or (b) they succeed, in which case a re-scoped manuscript — "fresh self-state rescues this model; gating is necessary; prediction and untuned classical control are insufficient; pseudo-pulse controls are required for state-dependent perturbation tests" — remains a rigorous and useful contribution, and arguably a more honest map of the computational structure. The negative-result discipline throughout the packet gives me confidence that the authors will report whichever outcome emerges.
