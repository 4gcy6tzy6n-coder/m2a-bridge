# Reviewer 3 (lens: NMI fit and AI relevance)

## Overall assessment

This manuscript studies what information a delayed feedback controller needs to remain stable, using a stylized two-pathway Drosophila steering model (ported from Canelo et al. 2024) plus three public-dataset analyses, and reports a methodological result about state-dependent perturbation tests being confounded by behavioral autocorrelation (pseudo-pulse control; Empirical layer III, claims C45–C46). The strongest and most honest part of the package is the computational necessity-structure analysis within the model: alternatives are enumerated and eliminated under matched information (command copy C18; Taylor and Smith-style delayed-only observers C37, C39; matched-budget instantaneous PD C40), and a quantitative freshness threshold for the self-state signal is derived (20–30 ms; C36, Fig. 14a). The empirical layers are candidly null or inconclusive (C43; C44 at p=0.0503 with CI crossing zero; C46 "NOT established"), and the Discussion states plainly that "computation has outpaced observation."

My remit is journal fit and AI relevance, and on that axis the paper is not yet an NMI paper. The central claim — the title's "necessary information for delayed feedback control," and the abstract's assertion that neither "trivial extrapolation" nor "a full forward-model observer" rescues stability — is established only inside a single fly-derived toy model, against deliberately simple estimators (C39 caveat: the observer's "internal model omits the delay in its feedback loop (naive forward model); DDE-aware observer untested"; C37 caveat: "only a trivial Taylor estimator; a perfect internal-model observer is an untested upper bound") and one untuned classical comparator (C40 caveat: "PD not gain-tuned"). There is no demonstration on any artificial control task, no second plant, no formal stability result (the Limitations note that the linear stability analysis "was attempted but not used"), and the reference list contains no control-theory or ML literature against which the contribution can be located. As written, this reads as a computational neuroscience paper with an unusually rigorous negative-result discipline — valuable, but the AI-facing case (a general design principle for delayed-feedback intelligent systems, and a transferable perturbation-analysis methodology) cannot be established from the present packet.

## Who would be interested and why

- **Computational neuroscientists working on sensorimotor control and efference copy**: the exhaustive alternative-elimination within the model (C14, C18–C21, C37–C41), the window-artifact correction (C23), and the criterion-sensitivity disclosures (C10, C33, C38) set a high bar for honesty.
- **Control theorists interested in delay systems**: the empirical split between "fresh state in a fast path" and "estimation from delayed observation" touches classic predictor-feedback questions; a subset will read it specifically to see whether naive Smith-style estimation was fairly characterized (currently it was not; see R3-M2).
- **ML researchers in embodied control and RL under observation delay**: the freshness dose-response (C36) is the kind of quantitative design constraint (a maximum tolerable sensor-pathway age) that could matter for artificial agents — but they will ask, so far unanswered, whether it transfers beyond this plant (R3-M1).
- **Methodologists of perturbation analysis**: the pseudo-pulse control and residual pipeline (C45–C46) are the most directly transferable idea in the paper for anyone running state-dependent treatment-effect tests on autocorrelated behavioral time series; this is the seed of a genuinely AI/methods-relevant contribution if generalized (R3-M5).

## Major strengths

1. **Matched-information elimination of alternatives inside the model.** The manuscript does not merely show that its favored architecture works; it builds the obvious competitors — a true command copy (C18, Fig. 6), a goal feedforward whose timing and content are decoupled (C34, Fig. 14b), a no-goal damping control (C35, Fig. 14c), an equal-information Taylor observer (C37), a Smith-style forward-model observer with exact and mismatched plant (C39), and a matched-budget instantaneous PD (C40) — and reports where each fails. This "necessity structure" methodology is exactly the right form for an intelligent-systems claim, even though its current scope is one model.
2. **Quantitative, testable design constraint.** The self-state freshness sweep (C36, Fig. 14a) converts the qualitative claim into a number: the rescue survives sample ages up to ~20–30 ms and collapses beyond. Prediction 6 then translates it into an experimental design ("delay the self-state pathway, not the visual scene"). For an NMI readership this is the most portable object in the paper.
3. **Disciplined self-correction.** The manuscript openly retracts its own earlier results: the 140 ms gating advantage was "a window artifact" (C23), the 200 ms critical delay is withdrawn, the source's zero-delay ranking is "not reproduced" (C12), and boundary-region failure rates are declared ensemble quantities (C38). The claim map's `retired_pre_revision` rows (C1–C3, C9, C15–C17) make the revision history auditable. This culture is rare and should be encouraged.
4. **The pseudo-pulse confound result.** Showing that a naive state-dependent perturbation test is defeated by generic behavioral autocorrelation — main effect +28.1 versus control-animal pseudo-pulses +20.1 (C45) — and that a residual pipeline shrinks but does not eliminate the floor (+6.9 floor, +8.6 excess, below the preregistered three-fold threshold; C46) is a real methodological finding with relevance well beyond flies.
5. **Preregistration and negative-result reporting throughout the empirical layers.** Inconclusive model selection (C43), a borderline predictive increment recorded unchanged (C44), and a below-threshold residual (C46) are all reported with their frozen controls. This is exemplary and should survive any revision.

## Major Concerns

### R3-M1
- **Severity**: High
- **Blocking**: Yes
- **Axis**: AI generality of the central claim
- **Claim pointer**: Title ("necessary information for delayed feedback control"); Abstract ("the ingredient that computationally stabilizes control is fresh current self-state"); claims C14, C19, C27, C30, C36, C40, C41
- **Evidence pointer**: Methods, Model ("The model follows the flight-simulator equations of Canelo et al. (2024)"); Limitations ("The model is a toy model. It is not connectome-constrained and does not include the fly's actual visual circuit"; "Parameters are taken from Canelo et al. (2024) and not fitted to new data"); claim-map caveats on C19 ("upper bound, not a biological mechanism")
- **Concern**: The paper's framing is general — what information is *necessary* for delayed feedback control — but every necessity result is internal to one second-order yaw plant with two hand-built pathways, one grating disturbance channel, and a threshold-crossing failure criterion that the authors themselves show moves the numerical boundary substantially (C33: gating critical delay "from 200 to 56 ms" under a different velocity threshold). No artificial control task, no second plant, no heterogeneous or state-dependent delays (Limitations: "Real sensorimotor delays are distributed and state-dependent"). An NMI readership will ask whether "fresh self-state in a fast path with a gated slow pathway" is a principle about delayed intelligent systems or a fact about this model, and the packet answers only the latter.
- **Why it matters**: NMI's core acceptance criterion for a modeling paper is that the contribution advances ML/AI or intelligent-systems design, not just one biological system. A single fly-derived toy model, however rigorously swept, cannot carry a title-level claim about delayed feedback control in general. This is the difference between "a careful model study" and "an NMI paper."
- **Resolution test**: Demonstrate the freshness/gating principle on at least one non-biological delayed-control benchmark with no fly-specific structure — e.g., a standard networked control plant, a delayed inverted pendulum, or a delayed-observation control/RL task — showing that the same intervention logic (fresh-state fast path present vs. aged vs. delayed-only estimation) predicts failure and recovery there, and that the freshness threshold scales with plant time constants in a predictable way. Absent this, the title and abstract must be narrowed to the specific model.

### R3-M2
- **Severity**: High
- **Blocking**: Yes
- **Axis**: Fairness of the estimator comparison underlying the title claim
- **Claim pointer**: Title ("not delayed extrapolation"); Abstract ("Neither trivial extrapolation nor a full forward-model observer built solely from delayed observations rescues stability"); claims C37, C39
- **Evidence pointer**: C39 caveat ("internal model omits the delay in its feedback loop (naive forward model); DDE-aware observer untested"); C37 caveat ("only a trivial Taylor estimator; a perfect internal-model observer is an untested upper bound"); Results, "Classical control alternatives" ("the observer we built does not embed the delay differential equation internally; these are boundaries of the claim"); Limitations bullet stating "The observer baseline tests only a trivial Taylor estimator; a perfect internal-model observer is an untested upper bound"
- **Concern**: Two problems compound. First, the negative claim about estimation is the paper's most AI-relevant assertion, and it is tested only against a second-order Taylor reconstruction and a Smith-style predictor that the authors themselves label naive (it omits the delay internally). Delay-aware predictor feedback and modern state-estimation approaches for systems with input/output delay are precisely the alternatives an NMI/control audience will name; the manuscript does not engage them, and the reference list contains no control-theory citations at all. Second, the Limitations section contradicts the Results: Limitations says "The observer baseline tests only a trivial Taylor estimator," while Results and C39 describe a Smith-style forward-model observer with exact and 10%-mismatched plant parameters (384 runs, P2-OBSERVER-02). Either the Limitations bullet is stale or the Results overstate the observer's sophistication; both cannot stand.
- **Why it matters**: If a competent delay-aware estimator does rescue stability, the title's central contrast collapses and the "fresh state is necessary" framing becomes an artifact of weak baselines — the classic ML failure mode of beating straw men. The internal inconsistency additionally signals to referees that the comparator suite was not fully reconciled across document layers.
- **Resolution test**: Either (a) implement at least one delay-aware estimator (observer integrating the delay internally, or predictor-feedback-style construction) and show the rescue still fails, with the same matched-budget discipline, or (b) weaken the title/abstract/Discussion to "delayed extrapolation and naive forward-model observers did not rescue stability in this model," and fix the Limitations/Results contradiction. Option (b) is acceptable if honestly scoped; the current wording is not.

### R3-M3
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Blocking**: No
- **Axis**: Semantics of "necessary" for an AI audience
- **Claim pointer**: Introduction ("computational necessity structure: which piece of information is actually required"); Abstract; Discussion ("the computationally necessary ingredient for surviving sensory delay is fresh current self-state")
- **Evidence pointer**: Limitations ("The linear stability analysis was attempted but not used because the delay-eigenvalue tracking was unreliable"); C17 (retired: linear stability numbers unreliable/censored); Methods, Statistics ("No inferential test is applied because the parameter grid is a design, not a random sample")
- **Concern**: "Necessary" is established by enumerating and eliminating a design space of alternatives, not by any bound, theorem, or argument that no other architecture class could succeed. The one attempt at formal grounding (linear stability) was abandoned. Within-model exhaustive elimination is a legitimate empirical method, but the manuscript's language ("required," "necessary ingredient") reads as a formal necessity claim. For an AI audience trained to distinguish "sufficient in our design space" from "necessary," this overreach will draw objections even from readers sympathetic to the result.
- **Why it matters**: The paper's value is highest when its claims are exactly as strong as its method. Overclaiming necessity invites rejection on grounds the authors could avoid by precise scoping.
- **Resolution test**: Restate the central claim with explicit quantifiers — e.g., "within this model class and the enumerated architectures, fresh self-state in a fast path is the only tested ingredient that restores bounded steering at the stated delay margins" — and either reinstate a formal argument for necessity (small-gain, information-theoretic, or delay-margin bound) or drop "necessary" from the title and Abstract in favor of a bounded sufficiency statement.

### R3-M4
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Blocking**: No
- **Axis**: Bio+AI narrative coherence and packaging
- **Claim pointer**: Title ("modeling and tests across four public neural-behavioral datasets"); Abstract (closing sentence: "Together the results define what action-related state information buys a delayed controller, where real circuits carry traces of it, and why demonstrating state-dependent perturbation effects requires controls..."); Discussion ("computation has outpaced observation")
- **Evidence pointer**: C43 ("INCONCLUSIVE_BY_RESOLUTION"), C44 ("p=0.0503; CI95 crosses zero"), C46 ("NOT established"); Empirical layer sections; Discussion summary paragraph
- **Concern**: The three empirical layers deliver, respectively: an inconclusive model selection, a borderline predictive increment that does not reach significance with a CI crossing zero, and a perturbation-specific effect that fails its preregistered threshold. The manuscript is commendably honest about this, but the title and abstract sell the empirical package ("tests across four public... datasets") as a co-equal pillar. For an NMI readership, the paper currently has the shape of a fly paper whose empirics failed, rather than an AI-methods paper whose biological case study sets constraints. The genuine AI-facing assets — the necessity-structure methodology, the freshness threshold, and the pseudo-pulse control — are under-led.
- **Why it matters**: Framing determines whether referees evaluate this as a contribution to intelligent-systems design (where the negative empirics are informative constraints) or as an incomplete biology story (where they are simply failures).
- **Resolution test**: Restructure the pitch so the AI-facing contribution leads the title/abstract (e.g., a design principle for delayed feedback control plus a transferable perturbation-analysis methodology), with the three dataset analyses framed explicitly as constraint-setting case studies of the principle; or add the artificial-task demonstration (R3-M1) that makes the empirical methodology's generality self-evident.

### R3-M5
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Blocking**: No
- **Axis**: Generality and ML positioning of the pseudo-pulse methodology
- **Claim pointer**: Empirical layer III closing paragraph ("Any future claim of state-dependent perturbation effects — in this or other systems — must report a pseudo-pulse control of the kind introduced here"; "the first quantified estimate of how far above that floor a real perturbation dataset currently sits"); C45, C46
- **Evidence pointer**: Methods additions ("Walking steering dataset... n=6 intervention animals" per C46 caveat context); C45 caveat ("capacity control not implemented in this round (disclosed)")
- **Concern**: The paper issues a field-wide prescription based on a demonstration in a single system with n=6 animals and one control suite, and does not connect the confound to the equivalent problems in ML: off-policy/counterfactual evaluation on autocorrelated sequences, state-dependent treatment effects in observational time series, or spurious state–response couplings in offline RL. The idea is transferable, but neither its transfer nor its ML-adjacent literature is present in the packet.
- **Why it matters**: The pseudo-pulse control may be the single most reusable contribution for the NMI readership; left as a fly-specific practice note, it forfeits that audience.
- **Resolution test**: Validate the pseudo-pulse/residual pipeline on at least one additional dataset where ground truth is known (synthetic interventions on held-out real behavior, or a second biological dataset with a genuine intervention axis), and add a Discussion passage relating the confound to state-dependent bias in offline/sequence-based evaluation in ML, with citations. Weaken the universal "must report" prescription to a recommendation for state-dependent perturbation tests on autocorrelated behavioral data until then.

## Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established

1. **Estimator class too weak for the negative claim** (C37, C39 caveats; "DDE-aware observer untested"). Until a delay-aware estimator is tested or the claim is weakened, the title's "not delayed extrapolation" contrast is not established. This is the single most load-bearing technical gap for the AI case.
2. **No demonstration beyond one plant** (Limitations: toy model, parameters not fitted, transport delay only). The freshness threshold (C36) and gating contribution (C41) have unknown portability; at minimum one artificial delayed-control task is needed to establish that the principle, not the model, is the contribution.
3. **Classical-control comparator under-tuned by design** (C40: "PD not gain-tuned"). A matched-gains PD is a fair *information* control but a weak *controller*; the Discussion acknowledges "a differently retuned classical controller was not excluded." Either add a tuned or delay-aware classical baseline or scope the claim accordingly (compounded by R3-M3's necessity language).
4. **No formal stability backing** (Limitations: linear stability "attempted but not used"; C17 retired with unreliable root tracking). Numerical failure-rate sweeps on a finite grid cannot support asymptotic language; if "stabilizes" is used in the title-level sense, a validated analytical or certified-numerical argument is needed, or the wording must stay explicitly finite-window ("failure at 2.5/6/10 s windows" as in Methods).
5. **Seed robustness of key sweeps is partial** (C20, C27, C28, C30: "1 seed, 8 parameter sets" for the self-state ablation, long-delay, fine-grid, and phase-diagram results; multi-seed coverage exists only for the command-copy/feedforward identity C21 and the classifier check C38). Given that the paper's own C38 shows label instability in a boundary region, single-seed cells at 250–500 ms (C27) and in the phase diagram (C30) should be multi-seed before they anchor Discussion-level claims.
6. **The pseudo-pulse pipeline has no ground-truth validation** (C45–C46, n=6). Recovery performance (does the residual pipeline find a planted state-dependent effect when one exists?) is untested; without an injection-recovery experiment on synthetic or semi-synthetic data, the method's sensitivity is unknown and its prescriptive framing (R3-M5) is premature.

## Assessment against Nature Machine Intelligence criteria

- **Originality**: Moderate. The necessity-structure methodology and the freshness dose-response are distinctive within computational neuroscience; the pseudo-pulse control is a genuinely new methodological idea. Neither is yet positioned against ML/control literature, and the packet contains no control-theory or ML references, which will read as a field-blindness problem at NMI.
- **Significance for ML/AI**: Unestablished as written. The design principle ("fresh self-state in a fast path, gated slow pathway") would be significant if it transferred; no transfer is shown (R3-M1), and the estimation negative is undercut by weak baselines (R3-M2). The perturbation-analysis methodology would be significant if generalized (R3-M5); currently it is one system at n=6.
- **Technical quality**: High within its self-imposed scope (preregistration, negative controls, claim map, window/criterion/step robustness checks, multi-seed spot checks), with the specific gaps listed above.
- **Breadth of interest**: Currently narrow (fly sensorimotor community plus a few delay-control readers). With R3-M1 and R3-M5 addressed, breadth would extend to embodied RL, networked control, and evaluation methodology.
- **Reproducibility**: Good in principle (code paths and run artifacts named per claim; frozen preregistration hashes listed; "DOI pending repository deposit" must be resolved at submission — Methods, Code and data availability).

## Recommendation posture

**Reject and resubmit (major revision).** I cannot recommend acceptance at Nature Machine Intelligence on this packet: the central case — a general statement about necessary information for delayed feedback control, of interest to the intelligent-systems community — cannot be established from a single fly-derived toy model and weakly specified estimator baselines (R3-M1, R3-M2, both blocking). I would actively encourage resubmission of an extended version: the existing computational analysis is unusually rigorous and honest, the freshness threshold is a strong portable object, and the pseudo-pulse control is a methodological contribution worth generalizing. A revision that (i) demonstrates the principle on at least one artificial delayed-control task, (ii) either tests a delay-aware estimator or narrows the title-level claim, (iii) repositions the paper's AI-facing contributions in the abstract, and (iv) validates the pseudo-pulse pipeline against ground truth, would be a credible NMI submission. Without those changes, the work is better suited to a computational neuroscience venue, where its discipline and negative-result hygiene are above-average strengths rather than partial answers to an unmet AI-standard bar.
