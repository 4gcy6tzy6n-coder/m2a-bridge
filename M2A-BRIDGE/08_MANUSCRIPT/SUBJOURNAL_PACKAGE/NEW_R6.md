### Empirical layer III: state-dependent perturbation responses — a confound and a partial fix

The sharpest test of action-related state dependence uses a dataset with a genuine intervention axis: walking animals carrying CsChrimson in a steering-related descending-neuron line, with intracellular membrane potential (4 kHz), ball-derived forward/lateral/yaw velocities (100 Hz), and a binary light-marker channel on aligned clocks. Each session contains hundreds of 500 ms light pulses; genetically matched controls carry no light. The question: does the response to the perturbation depend on the animal's behavioral state at the moment of perturbation, beyond stimulus history alone?

Our first, preregistered analysis compared a stimulus-history-only model against one adding strictly pre-pulse behavioral state, scored by held-out-animal log-likelihood. The primary endpoint passed its statistical threshold (5 of 6 animals positive; exact sign-flip p=0.031; Fig. 8; C45). The negative controls decided the interpretation. Applying the identical pipeline to random pseudo-pulse times in unstimulated control animals — where no perturbation-specific state dependence can exist — produced predictive gains of the same order (+20.1 versus the main +28.1 in median log-likelihood units). The apparent state dependence was generic behavioral autocorrelation: the current state predicts the next second of yaw whether or not a pulse occurs.

The second preregistered analysis attacked the confound directly. A baseline behavioral map from pre-pulse state to the subsequent yaw window was learned exclusively from non-pulse epochs and subtracted, so that the tested residuals could only carry pulse-related structure; the same three-way control suite (pseudo-pulse discrimination, time-shifted state, capacity-matched noise features) was frozen in advance. The residual pipeline did what it promised on the artifact: the control-animal floor fell from +20.1 to +6.9 median log-likelihood units, and the capacity control was null. The real-pulse residuals still carried a small, remarkably consistent excess — positive in all six animals, median +8.6, p=1/64, the smallest attainable value (Fig. 8; C46) — but this fell below the preregistered three-fold specificity threshold, and perturbation-specific state dependence remains **not established** in this dataset at this sample size (all intervention animals available were used; n cannot be expanded within the dataset).

We present these two rounds as one methodological result with a candidate signal attached. Tests of state-dependent perturbation responses are, at these behavioral timescales, dominated by a confound that survives naive controls; a residual pipeline removes most of it but leaves a floor that scales with the weakness of the baseline model. Any future claim of state-dependent perturbation effects — in this or other systems — must report a pseudo-pulse control of the kind introduced here. The small, consistent residual excess we record is, to our knowledge, the first quantified estimate of how far above that floor a real perturbation dataset currently sits, and provides the effect-size target for adequately powered future tests.
