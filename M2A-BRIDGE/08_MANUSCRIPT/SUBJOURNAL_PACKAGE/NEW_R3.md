### Classical control alternatives do not explain the rescue

The rescue described above depends on instantaneous self-state entering a fast feedback path. Two obvious objections must be answered before treating this as a statement about computation rather than about our particular implementation. First, perhaps any sufficiently good estimator built from the delayed observation could substitute for true instantaneous state. Second, perhaps ordinary classical control with instantaneous state, without any gating machinery, would suffice.

We tested both objections directly. As estimators we implemented a Taylor extrapolator (second-order reconstruction of current state from the delayed sample alone; Fig. 3, C37) and a forward-model observer in the style of a Smith predictor: an internal copy of the plant is integrated continuously, its self-state estimate corrected every delay interval by the innovation between the delayed measurement and the estimator's own delayed prediction. We ran the observer with the exact plant parameters and with a 10% mismatch in drag and inertia. Neither rescued stability. The Taylor-based feedforward failed from 50 ms onward, and the forward-model observer failed in all eight parameter sets at delays of 100 ms and above and in half the sets at 50 ms (Fig. 3; C39). As classical control we implemented instantaneous proportional-derivative bar stabilization that bypasses the delay entirely, using the same proportional and velocity gains and the same plant filter as the full model, with the optomotor pathway left ungated. This controller failed in 38-62% of parameter sets at every delay from 50 to 200 ms, while the gated goal-branch controller with the same instantaneous state stayed at 0% failure throughout (Fig. 3; C40).

Two conclusions follow, both bounded. Estimation from delayed observations — at any order we tested — did not substitute for fresh state in this system; the failure of the trivial Taylor estimator was not merely a matter of estimator sophistication. And gating contributed independently at matched information: with the goal branch and instantaneous state held fixed, the gated model failed 0% of the time while the ungated graded variant failed in 62-75% of parameter sets (C41). The narrower positive statement is that **fresh self-state in a fast path, with the delayed optomotor pathway gated during that engagement, is what stabilizes this model** — not goal content (pure derivative damping suffices; see below), and not any delayed-only estimator we could construct. We emphasize the matched-budget scope of the classical comparison: a differently retuned classical controller was not excluded, and the observer we built does not embed the delay differential equation internally; these are boundaries of the claim, listed again in the Limitations.
