## Discussion

Three layers of evidence now sit next to each other, and their relationship is the point.

In the model, the computationally necessary ingredient for surviving sensory delay is fresh current self-state routed through a fast feedback path, with gating of the delayed visual pathway contributing independently at matched information. The negative results are as important as the positive one. Estimators built from delayed observations failed at every order we tested — from second-order extrapolation to a forward-model observer with the exact plant dynamics — so the gap between delayed observation and fresh state cannot be closed by estimation sophistication within this system. Matched-budget classical proportional-derivative control with instantaneous state also failed, so the result is not merely "instantaneous state helps"; the architecture (fast path plus gated slow pathway) matters at the model level. These statements are internal to the model; they do not establish that any fly circuit implements this recipe.

In the data, the signatures are real but weaker than the model's necessity structure would suggest to a naive experimenter. Motor history incrementally predicts HS membrane potential in most animals, at a magnitude and significance level that supports "additional predictive information" and no more. Counter-evidence suppression is absent at the elementary motion detector and measured LPTC levels, consistent with the published localization and constraining where any graded-versus-gated competition could live. Behavioral dose-response shapes at n=19 cannot discriminate graded from gated families. And the one dataset with a genuine intervention axis showed that the most tempting analysis — does the perturbation response depend on behavioral state — is, at these timescales, an analysis of behavioral autocorrelation unless specifically designed otherwise; even a careful residual pipeline left the effect below a preregistered specificity threshold at n=6.

Read together, the honest summary is that computation has outpaced observation here. The model says what should be necessary; the available real datasets say that traces of action-related information exist but cannot yet be tied, with causal specificity, to the necessity structure. This gap is not an accident of sample size alone. It reflects the absence, in public datasets, of the experiment that would close it: manipulations of self-state pathway latency with simultaneous neural recording — the experiment our model's freshness threshold makes concrete and testable, and which we identified as absent from the literature in a systematic check. Our contribution in this layer is therefore threefold: a fully characterized computational necessity structure with its alternatives eliminated as far as the model allows; a set of empirical constraints and one consistent candidate signal, all reported with their controls; and a methodological result — the pseudo-pulse control and residual pipeline — that makes the next round of experiments, in this or other systems, measurably harder to fool.

The predictions that survive are specific. Freshness thresholds of 20-30 ms in the model translate into a manipulable experimental design (delay the self-state pathway, not the visual scene). The motor-history increment in HS predicts that, in datasets with more animals and normalized scales, the effect should replicate at a similar or larger magnitude. The residual excess above the pseudo-pulse floor (+2 to +5 log-likelihood units per animal in our data) is the effect size against which intervention datasets must now be powered. And the model's gating-independence result predicts that, at matched information, perturbations that disable gating should impair fast corrections even when instantaneous state remains available — a test the growing genetic access to pathway-specific inhibition makes feasible.

Limitations bound every layer. The model is a published minimal steering system, not a connectome; its delays, gains, and failure criteria involve choices, and we have shown the conclusions to be robust to the ones we tested while documenting those we did not (criterion sensitivity, observer internal-model fidelity, classical-controller tuning). The empirical analyses inherit the limitations of their datasets: sample sizes of 6-19 animals per question, one recording site per electrophysiology session, walking versus flight differences that forbid pooling, and the absence of any latency manipulation. Correlational predictive information, however carefully controlled, is not circuit causality, and we do not present it as such. All confirmatory analyses were preregistered with frozen files and hashes (Supplementary Table of preregistrations); exploratory analyses are labeled as such wherever they appear.
