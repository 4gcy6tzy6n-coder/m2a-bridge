### Empirical layer II: motor history predicts HS membrane potential incrementally

The model's rescue requires a fast, action-related signal. Does real visual-motor circuitry carry one? Simultaneous whole-cell HS recordings and wing-beat amplitudes during tethered flight answer a narrow version of this question: under identical visual stimulus structure (looming trials with and without turns), does the observable history of the animal's own movement predict membrane potential beyond what the visual stimulus history explains?

We froze a prediction protocol before analysis: strictly causal inputs (lags >= 20 ms), capacity-matched linear models on fixed raised-cosine bases, whole-animal cross-validation, and no selection on test data. In leave-one-animal-out validation across 12 animals, adding past motor history improved held-out prediction in 9 of 12 animals, with a median improvement of 0.056 in explained variance (Fig. 7; C44). The animal-level permutation test returned p=0.0503 with a bootstrap interval crossing zero. We record these numbers unchanged and interpret them, by prior agreement, as evidence that **observable motor history carries additional predictive information** for HS activity — nothing more. We do not claim an identified efference-copy source: the paper in question had already established that looming stimuli and movement-related signals both influence HS responses, our analysis cannot separate the two causal origins, and the absolute prediction quality is dominated by cross-animal scale mismatch (held-out variance-explained baselines were strongly negative, motivating the within-session normalization fixed for future use). The result is a consistency data point for action-related signals in a sensory pathway, not a mechanism discovery.
