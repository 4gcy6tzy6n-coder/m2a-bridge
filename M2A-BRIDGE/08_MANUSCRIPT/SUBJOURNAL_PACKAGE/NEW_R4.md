### Empirical layer I: benchmark reproduction and model selection on a visual counter-evidence dataset

We next asked what the model's central graded-versus-gated question looks like in real data. The counter-evidence dataset of Tanaka and Clark provides behavior and calcium imaging under stationary and flickering visual context — the situation in which visual evidence argues against one's own inferred motion. As a pipeline validation we reproduced, at the level of individual flies, the authors' precomputed population traces for three branches: Mi4 (behavior-related imaging branch), T4/T5 (elementary motion detectors), and five LPTC cell types. The reconstruction matched exactly for Mi4 (r=1.0, zero difference), reached r>=0.94 on all experimental epochs for T4/T5, and r>=0.85 on 11 of 12 epochs for LPTC (Fig. 5; C42). The reproduction also recovered the published qualitative facts: no consistent suppression by stationary foreground at the T4/T5 or LPTC levels (with enhanced responses in some LPTC conditions), and a real flicker-suppression signal — localizing behavioral counter-evidence suppression downstream of the measured stages.

With the pipeline anchored, we preregistered a confirmatory model selection on the behavioral contrast sweep: per-fly suppression-versus-contrast curves fitted with a graded Hill function (n=1) against a gated family (n in {2,4,8} selected inside nested cross-validation), scored on held-out flies with a parametric-bootstrap null. The result was inconclusive by a preregistered resolution rule: a median difference of -0.091 in held-out error with p=0.534 and a minimum detectable effect of 0.454, at n=19 flies whose across-fly variance dominated (Fig. 6; C43). An apparent gap seen during exploration vanished under refined fitting, and is recorded as a fitting artifact. We report this as what it is — the available sample cannot discriminate the shape families — and not as evidence for either side. The honest negative motivated the intervention-based analyses below: distributional shape comparisons at these sample sizes are resolution-limited, and stronger designs must come from experimental axes rather than more flexible curve families.
