# Cover letter draft — Track C (revised)

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **"Efference-copy gain, gating, and current self-state in a delayed fly steering model,"** for publication in *PLOS Computational Biology* as a Research Article.

The manuscript asks a narrow question about a widely used idea: how should a moving animal subtract the visual consequences of its own actions? Two families of models answer differently. Graded models subtract a continuous prediction of self-generated optic flow; all-or-none models suppress the stabilizing pathway while an object-driven turn is underway. Behavioral work in *Drosophila* has favored all-or-none gating under changes in background texture, but the two families have not been compared across efference-copy gains under an explicit transport delay.

We extend a published fly-steering model with an explicit visual feedback delay and compare five core variants with command-copy and goal-feedforward controls across efference-copy gains, gate thresholds, V-term treatments, simulation windows, and adaptive update rules. Three results survive these stress tests. First, gain. At the source model's fixed gain of 0.05, fixed-gain graded cancellation is inert and indistinguishable from no efference copy. At gains of 0.25 or more, delayed-sensory cancellation is harmful at 50 ms, failing in 100% of parameter sets versus 75% for no efference copy. Second, gating. At delays of 20 to 50 ms, gating on the filtered plant state prevents failure across gate thresholds from 0.01 to 0.5 (a second gating signal matches except one corner cell) and across clip, smooth, and unclipped velocity nonlinearities, while the continuous models fail in 62.5% to 100% of runs. The advantage is not asymptotic. At 100 ms it depends on the V treatment, and at 140 ms or longer it is not established. Third, adaptation. Neither the source-style squared-error update nor a signed-gradient update rescues failure at 50 ms. Two further checks complete the mechanism argument. A pre-plant-filter command copy does not rescue delay, matching the filtered plant state; only a current-visual oracle has lower long-delay failure, so the earlier benefit was an oracle effect. A goal feedforward branch that uses current proprioception does rescue: gating with a feedforward weight of 0.5 or more fails in no parameter set through 200 ms at both 6 and 10 s windows, robust to goal mismatch, and the rescue disappears when that branch's self-state estimate is delayed. What a delayed visual loop lacks, in this architecture, is current self-state rather than a copy of the issued command. The rescue extends to 500 ms at a full feedforward weight, survives torque noise two orders of magnitude above the main sweeps, and is documented with goal-mismatch, goal-delay, self-state, multi-seed, and ODE-equivalence controls. A second independent review round (three blind reviewers plus a forensic audit) has been addressed in full; the review, audit, and synthesis are included in the submission package.

The manuscript is explicit about what it does not show. It does not report new biological data, does not claim that the fly uses any of the model variants, and does not reproduce the source's zero-delay background ranking. Earlier claims about gating robust through 100 ms and about a linear-stability explanation were removed after stress-testing; the current paper reports the narrower results and the conditions under which they hold. We believe this is a useful, falsifiable contribution to the computational neuroscience of sensorimotor control.
